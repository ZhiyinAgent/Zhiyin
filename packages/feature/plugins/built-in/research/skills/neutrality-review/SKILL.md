---
name: neutrality-review
description: >-
  Use when evaluating contested or promotional material: filtering spin, identifying conflicting reports, and presenting perspectives in proportion to the evidence.
---

Approach the work as an editor responsible for fairness. Neutral does not mean giving every claim equal weight; it means representing each position accurately and in proportion to its evidence. Giving a fringe view the same space as a well-established one is false balance, not neutrality.

**Detect spin in sources:**
- Loaded or emotive wording where neutral terms exist.
- Selective statistics: missing denominators, cherry-picked time ranges, and relative changes without absolute ones. "Doubles the risk" may mean from 1 in 10,000 to 2 in 10,000.
- Vague attribution such as "experts say" or "studies show".
- Promotional material presented as independent analysis, including sponsored content and press releases.
- Framing through omission of relevant context, or through a headline the body does not support.

**Map the disagreement.** When reports conflict, identify exactly where they diverge: facts, definitions, methods, values, or predictions. Many disputes dissolve into different definitions; others are real. Values disagreements are not settled by evidence: present them as differing priorities.

**Find each side's best case.** Represent each significant perspective as its strongest proponents would state it, attributed to who holds it, with the evidence they rely on. With Tavily connected, tavily_search finds each side's own statements and tavily_extract reads them in full, so a position is quoted from its holders rather than from its critics. Distinguish mainstream positions from minority ones, and say which is which based on the evidence, not on volume: where they exist, systematic reviews, consensus statements of the relevant field and official statistics weigh more than individual studies or commentary.

**Write in neutral language:** describe rather than characterize, attribute contested claims, and keep your own assessment clearly labelled and separate. For example, "The company's reckless expansion collapsed" becomes "The company closed 40 of its 120 stores in 2025; its chief executive attributed this to rents, and two former managers to over-expansion", with a source for each part.

**Check your own output for balance:** the same standard of evidence applied to every side, no loaded words, and no position misrepresented. Read it once as each side would: would its holders recognise their view? The research/fact-checker specialist can check the figures and attributions it rests on.

**Report** the perspectives with their evidence, where the evidence is strong or weak, and what remains genuinely unresolved.
